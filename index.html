Senior full-stack engineer on the Research Informatics Core at Mass General Brigham, working in Ruby on Rails since 2021.

About

I spent the first decade of my working life backstage in New York, running scenic and properties departments for The Public Theater, Theatre for a New Audience and The New School. Theater teaches you to build complicated things on a fixed deadline with people who are counting on you, and to care about every detail the audience will never consciously notice. In 2021, I retrained as a developer and joined the Research Informatics Core at Massachusetts General Hospital.

Five years on, I'm a senior engineer on that same team, now part of Mass General Brigham. We're a small group that runs close to 40 production Rails applications for research offices, HR and innovation programs across the hospital system, and ten of them are mine. I've taken over legacy systems and carried them from Rails 5 to Rails 8, rewritten two of them, moved our applications from on-premises hosting to AWS, and designed and built three new applications and the team's Okta single-sign-on engine from an empty repository.

  • OCR

    2025 to now, sole developer

    Protocol submission and multi-stage review platform for the Office for Clinical Research. Designed and built from an empty repository; live since December 2025, with 200 study teams across 35 institutions submitting through it and every approved protocol synced to two downstream research-center systems over an API I also wrote.

    Rails 8.1Solid QueueStimulusDockerGitHub ActionsAWS
    What went into it
    • Versioned protocol templates: sections, fields and dropdown options are data, so the office can change a form without a deploy and existing protocols keep the version they were written on.
    • Reviews assigned automatically from the resources a study uses, then a comment round where the admin releases reviewer questions to the study team in batches with digest emails.
    • Routing by performing site, so MGH, BWH and affiliate protocols reach the right admin group, with institution-specific letters and standard attachments managed in the app.
    • Push integration to the CCI and TCRC systems: token-authenticated endpoints on their side, a retrying client and background sync job on mine.
    • Shipped with a Docker build and a GitHub Actions pipeline running RSpec, RuboCop, Brakeman and dependency audits on every push.

  • OAWA

    2026 to now, sole developer

    Facility inspection, findings and corrective-action tracking for the Office of Animal Welfare Assurance and its IACUC, covering 13,000 rooms. Built from scratch, including escalation rules, templated email and a nightly facility-data import.

    Rails 8.1Solid QueueHotwireRSpec
    What went into it
    • Inspection cycles over buildings, areas and rooms, with the master list of facilities imported from the Insight space system and the rest derived from it.
    • Findings with deficiency categories and quick picks, corrective-action templates, response tracking and escalation rules that fire when a deadline passes.
    • Email templates with distribution lists and recipient roles, previewed before sending, so the office writes its own notices.
    • IACUC member and alternate management tied to login accounts, and a 140-file RSpec suite from day one.

  • LabSec

    2025 to now, lead developer

    Laboratory safety inspection and compliance system covering 20,000 rooms and 4,000 PI sites across five institutions, with about 400 inspections a year. I led its rewrite from an Ember front end into a single Rails application: surveys and events, corrective actions and reminders, and a space-data sync with the facilities system.

    Rails 7.2 to 8.1StimulusesbuildPundit
    What went into it
    • Moved the back end from Rails 6.1 and Ruby 2.7 to Rails 7.2 and Ruby 3.3 first, then rebuilt the Ember screens as server-rendered Rails views with Stimulus.
    • Inspection surveys and events with present-observer tracking, violation counts and finalization rules.
    • Action items and follow-ups with reminder and past-due notifications, scoped by PI site.
    • A nightly Insight import with retries and staleness marking, so the lab roster never silently drifts from the facilities record.

  • Evals

    2022 to now, primary developer

    The enterprise performance-evaluation system: 260,000 employee records across 30 institutions, about 40,000 evaluations a year and more than 140,000 logins over five months, synced with HR identity data. Employee search, a rewritten bulk importer, optimized notification jobs and the PeopleSoft-to-Workday transition.

    Rails 8.1Delayed JobMySQL
    What went into it
    • About 40,000 evaluations a year run through the workflow rules, second-evaluator logic and reporting-structure checks I added, on a record set of 260,000 employees. Sessions average ten minutes and seven pages, so the review screens get real use.
    • Rewrote the bulk employee-record importer and the identity sync so the overnight load finishes in a fraction of the time.
    • Carried it from Rails 5.1 through 6, 7 and 8, clearing the deprecations at each step.
    • Reworked notifications, HR links and countdown reminder emails when the organization moved from PeopleSoft to Workday.

  • okta_sso

    2026, sole author

    The team's Okta single-sign-on Rails engine: OIDC authorization code with PKCE, token verification against JWKS, outage fallback and a per-app cutover check. Now used by every application we run.

    Rails engineOIDCJWT
    What went into it
    • Authorization-code flow with PKCE and ID tokens verified against Okta's published keys, with no email identifiers ever sent to the provider.
    • A break-glass local login that stays available if Okta is down, and RP-initiated logout so signing out of an app signs out of Okta too.
    • A preflight report per application that lists accounts that would fail the cutover, so each app could be switched over cleanly.
    • Folded into the shared authentication engine beside the existing LDAP path, with setup and cutover documentation for the Okta admin team.

  • NHPCSG

    2023 to now, sole developer

    Grant application, review and funding-round portal for a nonhuman-primate research consortium. My first application built solo from inception.

    Rails 8.1caxlsxwicked_pdf
    What went into it
    • Funding rounds with application forms, review templates, scoring and steering-committee access, with the round lifecycle automated.
    • External accounts for reviewers outside the hospital directory, alongside LDAP for staff.
    • Excel and PDF exports for the committee, and a maintainability pass that replaced CodeClimate with qlty.

  • iSuggest and EDC

    2022 to now, maintainer

    An enterprise suggestion platform I rewrote, with a Microsoft Graph inbox integration, and the Ether Dome Challenge innovation-contest site, with 11,000 registered participants across eight contests, which I keep current through each framework upgrade.

    Rails 8.1Microsoft GraphBootstrap
    What went into it
    • iSuggest: notification preferences, reminder schedules, role consolidation, a status workflow, a visual refresh and a Sass migration, then Rails 8.1.
    • EDC: made the contest site configurable per contest for new programs, replaced an end-of-life editor with TinyMCE, and carried it from Rails 6 to 8.
    • Added Brakeman and RuboCop to both pipelines and cleared every finding they raised.
